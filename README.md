## Data & Business Analyst

For twenty years I sold and managed relationships in Brazilian retail banking. The job was always the same: find the leak. The account that stopped moving, the product that stopped selling, the number that didn't add up — and then convince someone to act on it.

I now do that with data. **Every project below ends in a decision, not a dashboard.**

The four repositories are one series, ordered the way an analytics function matures — from *can we trust the data* to *what should we do*.

<img src="assets/regra_60_30_10.png" width="100%" alt="60/30/10 colour proportion of the Synthwave Horizon system">

### 1 · Can the data carry the question?

**[callcenter-data-quality-audit](https://github.com/msitta/callcenter-data-quality-audit)**

A telephony company wanted its "inefficient" operators ranked. Across 53k call records I showed the ranking could not be built fairly: corrupted call durations, a missed-call flag that measured something else entirely, and 12 of the "operators" turning out to be automated dialers.

→ *Fix collection at the source before evaluating anyone.*

### 2 · Where is value leaking?

**[callcenter-revenue-leakage](https://github.com/msitta/callcenter-revenue-leakage)**

A 100-agent operation simulated from scratch to practise financial diagnosis. Traced USD 46.6k/month leaking through system-dropped calls and short-call gaming, and costed the fix.

→ *Renegotiate the dialer contract; monitor sub-15-second calls.*

### 3 · Where should the budget go?

**[marketing-roi-cohort-analysis](https://github.com/msitta/marketing-roi-cohort-analysis)**

LTV by cohort, CAC by channel, ROI. The channel taking most of the budget had the highest acquisition cost and the lowest customer value.

→ *Cut Source 3, reallocate to Sources 1 and 2.*

### 4 · Who is about to leave?

**[gym-churn-prediction](https://github.com/msitta/gym-churn-prediction)**

26% monthly churn. Logistic Regression at 85.4% recall, chosen over Random Forest because missing a leaver costs more than a false alarm. K-Means isolated two clusters carrying 52% and 44% churn.

→ *Four retention programmes, targeted at those clusters.*

Chapters 1 and 2 share a domain on purpose: the same call-centre operation, two different questions.
